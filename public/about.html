About us

Our history

Scouting reached the Ismaili community within a generation of its birth. This is the story of how it grew — and the people who guided it.

Origins

From Brownsea Island to Karachi

The Scout Movement began in August 1907, when Robert Baden-Powell took twenty boys to camp on Brownsea Island off the south coast of England. His book Scouting for Boys followed in 1908 and the idea spread across the world with remarkable speed — reaching the Indian subcontinent within a few years.

In the Ismaili community of Karachi, three troops were established in quick succession: the Prince Aly S. Khan Kharadar Scout Troop (1927), the Prince Aly S. Khan Garden Scout Troop (1927) and the Vazir Rahim Boarding Scout Troop (1928). They joined a community that already ran a pipe band, volunteer corps, social workers and khidmatgars — so the ethic of uniformed service was already familiar.

Baden-Powell and the first Scouts, Brownsea Island, 1907. Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.

1930 — a movement is born

Prince Aly Khan, our first Chief Scout

In 1930, under the guidance of the 48th Imam, Mawlana Sultan Mahomed Shah, Aga Khan III, his son Prince Aly Salman Khan formally introduced Scouting to the Ismaili community of the subcontinent. He was appointed the first Ismaili Chief Scout in 1931 and held the post until his death on 13 May 1960.

The movement was organised in two sections — the Boy Scouts (today the Scout Unit) and the Wolf Cubs (today the Shaheen Unit) — a structure that still shapes our units.

Timeline

Milestones

  1. 1907

    The first Scout camp

    Robert Baden-Powell camps with 20 boys on Brownsea Island, England. Scouting for Boys is published the following year.

  2. 1927

    First Ismaili troops

    The Prince Aly S. Khan Kharadar and Garden Scout Troops are formed in Karachi; the Vazir Rahim Boarding Scout Troop follows in 1928.

  3. 1930

    The movement is formally begun

    On the Hidayat of Aga Khan III, Prince Aly Khan introduces Scouting to the Ismaili community, with Boy Scout and Wolf Cub sections.

  4. 1931

    First Chief Scout

    Prince Aly Khan is appointed Chief Scout of the Ismaili Scouts, a role he holds for almost three decades.

  1. 1960

    Passing of Prince Aly Khan

    The first Chief Scout dies on 13 May 1960.

  2. 1965

    Prince Amyn Mohamed named Chief Scout

    Mawlana Hazar Imam appoints his brother, Prince Amyn Mohamed, as the second Chief Scout through a Talika Mubarak.

  3. 1974

    Investiture in Karachi

    On 7 December 1974 Prince Amyn is formally invested as Chief Scout before some 3,000 uniformed members and 10,000 members of the Jamat. The association by then counts 11 Rover Crews, 17 Scout Troops, 15 Cub Packs, 6 Ranger Companies and 16 Girl Guide Companies — 3,525 members.

  4. Today

    Across Pakistan and the world

    Ismaili District Boy Scouts Associations in Gilgit, Hunza, Chitral and the Central Region, registered with the Pakistan Boy Scouts Association, alongside Aga Khan Scouts & Guides groups in the UAE, Canada and beyond.

Our home ground

Scouting in the mountains

In Gilgit-Baltistan and Chitral, Scouting is woven into community life. The Ismaili District Boy Scouts Association Hunza reports that more than 85% of children and young people aged 5–25 in its area are members — perhaps the highest participation of any Scouting body in Pakistan.

Units run winter and summer camps, youth forums, pioneering and camp-craft training, parades and large camp assemblies, and are on the front line whenever the community needs help — from earthquakes to landslides to floods.

Values

The Scout Promise & Law, our way

Duty to God and community

Faith and ethics are the foundation. Service to the Jamat and to wider society is how we live them out.

Friendship & non-violence

A Scout is a friend to all and a brother or sister to every other Scout — in thought as well as action.

Service before self

Personal interest comes second to the welfare of others. It is the motto that ties every generation of Ismaili Scouts together.
